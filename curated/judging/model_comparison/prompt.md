fields such as artificial intelligence, human-computer interaction, computer science, cognitive
psychology, linguistics, neuroscience, communication, sociology, philosophy and history of
science). Each item shows a target word, the parts of speech it is studied in, a sentence with the
target marked between ⟨t⟩ and ⟨/t⟩, and the two programs' readings of the marked word, labelled A
and B in an order that varies from item to item:

- its part of speech (NOUN, VERB, ADJ, PROPN and so on);
- its lemma (the dictionary form: *networks* as *network*, *modelled* as *model*);
- the name it sits in, if any, with the kind of name (ORG, PERSON, WORK_OF_ART and so on).

The readings differ in a way that changes whether the word counts as a use: a part of speech it is
or is not studied in, a lemma that is or is not the target, a name or no name.

Answer which reading is right about what decides the count:
- `A`: A is right and B is wrong;
- `B`: B is right and A is wrong;
- `both`: both are right about what decides the count (they differ only in a detail that does not
  change it);
- `neither`: both are wrong.

A name is the name of a person, organisation, place, product, event, law, named test or scale, or a
title; a term of the field is not a name (*neural network*, *working memory*). When the target is
one part of a hyphenated word, only the name decides the count.

Use the sentence and your knowledge of English; do not look anything up and do not run code.
Answer for each item with its id, your answer, and a few words of explanation.
