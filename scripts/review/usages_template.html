Yes, a use

  • The target word in a part of speech the item lists, in any sense, inflection or spelling (networks; behaviour for behavior).
  • Part of a hyphenated word, in any part of speech (self-attention, attention-based).
  • A noun before another noun is still a noun (network analysis).
  • A generic term, even capitalised (Artificial Intelligence).
  • The abstract's own title and headings, and the word mentioned as a word (the term "attention").

No

  • Inside a name: a person, organisation, place, product, event, law, named test or scale, one specific named system or model, or the title of another work that is cited (Cable News Network).
  • A part of speech the item does not list, such as a verb where only the noun counts (they were networking).
  • Another word: an abbreviation, the letters inside a longer word (attentional for attention), code or a file name, or OCR damage; or the highlight cuts the word.
  • The sentence is not English.
