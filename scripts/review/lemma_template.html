Which lemma does this word take?

Before counting words, the project shrinks every plural and every verb ending in -s to its base word, so senses and sense count as one word. For each word below, two programs guessed the base word; pick the right guess, or type it if both are wrong. The example sentences only show what the word means; they are not being checked. Part 1 is blind. On a phone, tap a card; on a keyboard, 1 or 2 pick a guess, 3 leaves the word as it is, 4 types another word, arrows move. Part 2 opens when part 1 is done: a second look at the forms where you and the agent judges differ, then the agents' most frequent calls on spaCy's own merges, with their answer suggested (Enter takes it).

How to answer, with examples

Choose the word it should be counted as, given what it means in the sentences. The agent judges followed the same rules. None of the example words is on the page.

  1. An ordinary plural or verb takes its singular or base form. theories → theory, applies → apply; a plural acronym takes the acronym, apis → api. Press the key of that suggestion, 1 or 2: their order is random.
  2. Keep the word when its -s is not a plural or verb ending 3: names (hobbes, athens), words that end in -s in the singular or have no singular (basis, thesis, news), and foreign or technical terms that are not English plurals. If hobbes is offered only hobbe, press 3.
  3. Keep a plural that is a word of its own 3 when the singular, as this corpus mostly uses it, means something else: means in "by means of a survey" stays, as mean here mostly means average, or to intend. Merge when the singular means the same or a closely related thing: networks → network.
  4. Neither suggestion is right 4: type the lemma. If focuses is offered only focuse, press 4, type focus and press Enter.

Text-extraction junk or a fragment of a split word: keep it as it is, 3.

Sentences with different meanings. Your answer applies to every use of the word, not just these sentences, so answer for the meaning most of them use. With arms, "raised their arms" counts as arm but "the arms race" stays arms: if two of the three sentences are about weapons, leave it, 3. If both meanings shrink to the same word, there is nothing to choose.
