Each record should hold a work's abstract. For each one, say whether the text is the work's own prose: the authors writing about their own work, in English. Judge the text, not its quality.

  • Abstract: an abstract or summary, with or without headings; the opening paragraphs of an article; a book review, an editorial, a comment or a reply; an abstract of a talk, thesis, report or chapter; a short abstract or one cut off before its end; an abstract with small leftovers around it (a copyright line, a stray reference, damaged words). A record holding the work's own prose and a block of other material counts as an abstract here.
  • Junk: not the work's own prose: a citation line or author and journal details, a list of keywords; a publisher's or repository's page; a table of contents or a list of titles; a description of an archive item; a quotation or text from another work; a letter to the editor, an obituary, a correction notice or a news item; a fragment with no complete sentence; text mostly not in English.
  • Unsure: you can't tell; say why in the note.
