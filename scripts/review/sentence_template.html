Where did the splitter cut?

Check only where the cuts fall, not the text itself: spelling, spacing and OCR noise don't matter.

How to judge
  • Part 1, whole abstracts. Each numbered row is one sentence as cut; each dashed strip between two rows is a cut.
  • A wrong cut falls where no sentence ends: after an abbreviation (p., et al., e.g.), an initial or a number, or inside brackets. Press Wrong cut on its strip.
  • A missed break: two sentences left in one row. Tap the word that should start the second one; tap again to undo.
  • Headings glued to the start of a sentence are not errors (Abstract, Methods:, Background:): leave them.
  • Bullets and headings between sentences start new units. A row holding several bullet items, or a heading standing between two sentences, has a missed break at each further unit: tap its first word. In text that isn't prose, such as reference lists or catalogue lines, mark only the cuts that are plainly wrong.
  • Then press No mistakes or Save marks (or Enter).
  • Part 2, single sentences. The highlighted sentence is right when both its cuts fall where a sentence ends and it holds one sentence (a heading at its start is fine). Otherwise say whether its start is wrong, its end is wrong, or it holds two sentences. Keys 1 to 5.
