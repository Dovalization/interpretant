# Devlog 06 — Senses, Known Answers, and a Rule to Stop

*November 2026*

---

Devlog 05 ended with the direction of HCI's change taken out of the README. The last round on
it, W, came back untestable. Its guard check compared each planted move with zero instead of
with the same seed's unplanted text, my design error, and the numbers behind the untestable
verdicts showed both instruments agreeing, which is the opposite of what I had bet. By the
rule I wrote before it, no further round on HCI's direction followed.

Looking back over those rounds, the problem was not any single measure. Nothing outside the
corpus says which way HCI moved, so every round ended up testing the previous round's
instrument rather than the world. The one finding that has held since the redesign,
*embodiment* meaning two different things in HCI and philosophy, is of another kind: it is
about a word, you can read its evidence, and it matches what people in both fields would say.

## Back to words, at a finer grain

A word vector per field and decade blends a word's senses. *Information* in psychology mixes
information processing with health information. So I built the instrument the roadmap had
promised since devlog 04: encode every occurrence of a word in its sentence with a contextual
model, cluster all fields' and decades' occurrences together, and read each cell's share of
each sense (Giulianelli and colleagues did this in 2020).

The lesson from the direction rounds decided the order. Before the instrument could answer an
open question, it had to find changes whose history is documented. In AI, *attention* gained
the attention mechanism of neural networks in the mid-2010s, and psychology's *attention* kept
its cognitive sense. AI's sense of *intelligence* spread into other fields' abstracts with
generative AI. Thirty control words drawn at random checked that *attention* stood out from
typical words, and two encoders, SciBERT and ModernBERT, were compared.

## Three calibrations

**Round C** passed two of five predictions under both encoders, the same two. AI's *attention*
changed about twice as much as the largest of the thirty controls, and AI's *intelligence* rose
in communication, sociology, philosophy and history and philosophy of science. But the
silhouette, which chose the number of senses, picked two, and the two were not mechanism and
cognition: they were attention that operates (the transformer mechanism together with
selective attention and ADHD) and the stock phrase of academic prose, *particular attention is
given*. One of my bets was simply wrong: cognitive psychology's 2020s abstracts write about
artificial intelligence too, ChatGPT's creativity measured against people's.

The encoders agreed to within a few hundredths, so I kept SciBERT, fixed eight senses after
trying the split on *attention*'s data, and repaired some data on the way: abstracts mostly in
another script are skipped (one of them crashed the first run), URLs are removed, plurals
count, and two fields are compared as communities only if OpenAlex does not nest one inside the
other. A label-noise check showed that most abstracts a classifier places in another field are
not mislabelled; computer science contains AI and HCI, and psychology contains cognitive
psychology. And because *attention* was now seen, the test had to be a word the instrument had
not met.

**Round C′** used *hallucination*: a perception without a stimulus in psychology and
neuroscience, a language model's fabrication in AI's 2020s abstracts. The finer split worked
on *attention* in every seed. On *hallucination* the instrument found clean language-model
senses every time, but in two seeds the rule that grouped senses into one meaning, the fewest
senses holding 80% of the marked uses, needed a mixed sense to get there, and took it along
with psychology's perceptual uses.

**Round C″** repaired that rule on the data already seen and tested it on *prompt*. I wrote
into its predictions that it was the last calibration. It failed both ways: too narrow when
the uses of *prompt engineering*, a phrase I had chosen as a mark, formed a sense so pure it set
the bar out of reach, and too broad when senses mixing old dialogue-system prompts with
psychology's cleared a lower bar.

## What stopping means

Across the three rounds the encoder placed every known change where its history puts it. What
never held on a case it was not chosen on was the step from clusters to one meaning's share,
and each version of that step leaned on marks I had chosen by hand. The instrument was partly
supervised without admitting it. A fourth round would have been the pattern the stopping rule
exists for: adjusting the instrument after each failure until something passes.

So the sense rounds end as scored. The README says the instrument did not pass calibration,
and what it showed is exploratory: AI's *intelligence* spreading into other fields, the
language-model sense of *hallucination* appearing in psychology's abstracts. If I come back to
senses, it will be with a supervised instrument, hand-labelled occurrences and a classifier on
the vectors, calibrated from scratch. The word-targeted cells downloaded for these rounds and
the label-noise measurement stay on disk for that.

## Where it stands

The robust finding is unchanged: *embodiment*'s two senses, with *intentionality*'s stability
beside it. HCI changed between the 1990s and the 2020s, and no direction is claimed. Sense-level
drift was tried and stopped by its own rule. Next:

- the explorer online, with round M's flagged cells marked;
- a write-up of what the direction rounds taught about measuring movement between fields:
  counting a classifier's assignments can report a move for a field that only changes more
  than the rest, and the same classifier read through its probabilities points the other way.
