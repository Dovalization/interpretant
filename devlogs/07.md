# Devlog 07 — The Test I Ran Last

*November 2026*

---

Devlog 06 ended with sense-level drift stopped by its own rule. Before deciding what came next,
I read the whole record again from the first prediction: five rounds on the embedding grid,
fourteen on the language models and the classifier, three on senses. Twenty-two rounds, 118
verdicts, 57 passed, 53 falsified, 8 untestable as written.

Two things stood out. Everything that held was about a word and could be read: *embodiment*'s
neighbours in HCI and in philosophy are lists anyone can check against what people in those
fields would say. And almost every round that compared whole fields ended up finding something
about the measure rather than the fields. That second pattern I already knew. The first one
raised a question I had not asked.

## The question

The sense rounds had taught me to check an instrument on changes with a documented history
before trusting it with an open question. I had done that for the new instrument. I had never
done it for the old one. Every robust finding rests on the embedding grid, and the grid had
been checked only against itself: control words, a second training seed, a second method, a
rebuilt corpus. All of those say the grid is consistent. None of them says it can see a change.

And there was a warning in the record from the very first round. I had predicted that *network*
in AI would be flagged at the connectionist turn of the late 1980s. It was not. I filed that as
a wrong guess about *network*. It was just as much a fact about the grid, and I did not look at
it again for four rounds.

## Round 5

So I gave the grid four changes whose dates and fields are documented: *attention* in AI's
2020s, the attention mechanism of neural networks; *platform* in communication's 2010s, social
media; *network* again, at AI's connectionist turn; and *alignment* in AI's 2020s, aligning
language models, which I rated the weakest case because the 2020s also talk about cross-modal
alignment. I added two transitions where nothing documented happened, and asked that the new
sense of *attention* show up between AI and psychology too. The predictions went into git
before I looked at a single rank, with a line saying no round would follow to repair the grid,
whatever happened.

It flagged one of the four, *alignment*, the case I trusted least. *attention* was a near miss:
its neighbours changed more than almost any word of its frequency, but its vector moved just
under the bar. *platform* and *network* did not register at all. Neither quiet transition was
flagged, and the control words have stayed quiet in every round.

That is a particular kind of instrument: cautious, not noisy. When it says a word changed,
believe it. When it says nothing changed, that means very little. *Embodiment* comes out of this
stronger, because a positive finding from a cautious instrument is worth more. *Intentionality*
comes out weaker. I had listed it as stable in philosophy for four rounds, and stability was
only ever the grid not flagging it. It is now "no change detected", and the README says why.

## The write-up

The most useful thing the project has produced so far is not a claim about academic fields.
It is the record of what three instruments did when they were finally asked to find something
we already knew, so I wrote that up as an essay, *Calibrate before you measure*
(`docs/known_answers.md`). It has the numbers and two figures; here is the short version:

- **Agreement is not accuracy.** Seeds, second methods and encoders that agree tell you an
  instrument is reliable. Only a known answer tells you it measures what you think.
- **Run the known-answer test first.** I ran it last for the grid, after building every finding
  on it, and it changed what the project claims. For the sense instrument I ran it first, and it
  stopped the instrument before it said anything about an open question.
- **Commit a stopping rule, not just predictions.** The direction question ran for thirteen
  rounds without one, each repairing the last. The sense work had one and stopped after three.
- **Keep the misses.** My bets were worse than the method all along, and the misses, written
  down, became the next round's better predictions.

None of this is new to anyone who calibrates instruments for a living. It was new to me in
practice, and the ledger is what let me see it.

## Where it stands

Robust: *embodiment*'s two senses, and the way a subfield's language predicts its parent's
better than the reverse. Philosophy's model came to predict HCI's abstracts better, one way
only, because HCI dropped its 1990s interface vocabulary. HCI changed between the 1990s and the
2020s, and I claim no direction. *Intentionality*: no change detected. Sense-level drift: tried,
stopped. Next:

- the explorer online, with the cells the held-out check flagged marked, and *intentionality*
  shown for what it is;
- any new instrument, or any new use of the grid, starts with a few known answers and a
  stopping rule committed beside its predictions.
